5 Best Free Gallery Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated November 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Every Android phone ships with a gallery — and many of them quietly push your photos to the cloud, show ads, or bury your albums behind a backup prompt. We tested the most popular free gallery apps on a mid-range Android phone for one question: which one shows your photos quickly and privately, without ads or a cloud subscription?

What we tested

  • Offline first — your photos open instantly with no internet needed
  • Ads & upsells — whether the app interrupts you with ads or backup prompts
  • Organisation — albums, folders, favourites, hidden folders and search
  • Privacy — what leaves your phone, and what stays on it

1. Fossify Gallery — best fully free gallery

Fossify Gallery is the open-source successor to Simple Gallery, and it is the rare gallery that is genuinely free: no ads, no cloud backup prompts, no in-app purchases. It opens fast, shows photos by folder or album, has a hidden-folder option and a built-in photo editor for basic crops and filters. For most people, this is the answer — install it and forget about it.

2. Google Photos — best if you want backup and search

Google Photos is already on most phones, and its search ("show me photos of the beach") and automatic backup are genuinely useful. The catch: backup storage counts against your Google account quota, and the app keeps nudging you toward a paid storage plan. If you already pay for Google storage, it is hard to beat; if you don't, the free tier fills up faster than you expect.

3. Samsung Gallery — best if you already have it

Samsung phones ship with a solid gallery: clean albums, a recycle bin, a secure folder for private photos and decent built-in editing. If your phone already has it, check it before installing anything else — it does the basics well with no ads. It is not available as a general download for other brands, so non-Samsung owners should look elsewhere on this list.

4. Aves Libre — best for large photo libraries

Aves Libre is a free, open-source gallery built for people with thousands of photos. It loads huge folders quickly, supports tags, smart albums and map view, and shows image metadata without fuss. The interface is more functional than pretty, but if your camera roll is enormous, its speed is worth it.

5. Piktures — best looking gallery

Piktures brings a clean, modern design with a calendar view that shows your photos on the days you took them, plus a secure drive for private shots. The free tier covers everyday browsing well; a few extras (like the secure drive beyond a small limit) need a one-time payment.

Our verdict: install Fossify Gallery first — it is free forever, shows your photos offline with no ads and no cloud nagging. Only use Google Photos if you specifically want backup and search and are happy paying for storage when the free quota runs out. Avoid "free" gallery apps that demand cloud backup before they will even show your camera roll.
