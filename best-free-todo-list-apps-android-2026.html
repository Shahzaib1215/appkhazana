5 Best Free To-Do List Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated November 2, 2026 · 7 min read

A to-do app only works if you actually open it. We tested the most popular free task managers on a mid-range Android phone for one question: which one makes it easiest to capture a task in seconds, remind you at the right time, and keep a busy day under control — without locking the basics behind a subscription?

What we tested

  • Quick capture — how fast a task goes from thought to list, including widgets and voice input
  • Reminders — due dates, recurring tasks and location-free time reminders on the free tier
  • Organisation — lists, priorities, subtasks and search when the list grows
  • Free-tier honesty — what is genuinely free, and what quietly needs a paid plan

1. Microsoft To Do — best free task manager overall

Microsoft To Do is the rare task app whose free tier covers almost everything most people need: lists, due dates, reminders, recurring tasks, subtasks, file attachments and a clean "My Day" view for planning each morning. It syncs with a Microsoft account and works well on the web too, so a list started on your phone is waiting on your computer. There is no paid tier to upsell you into — that alone puts it ahead of most rivals here.

2. Google Tasks — best simple, fully free option

If every task app you have tried felt like too much app, Google Tasks is the antidote. It is a plain list with due dates, subtasks and reminders, tightly integrated with Google Calendar and Gmail. There are no projects, tags or collaboration features to learn — and nothing to pay for. The trade-off is real: no priorities, no rich organisation, and no widget as flexible as the competition's. For a personal daily list, though, simple wins.

3. Todoist — best for power users (with a free-tier catch)

Todoist has the best task language of any app here: type "Call Ammi every Monday at 9am" and it creates a recurring task with the date set. Its projects, labels, filters and karma streaks suit people juggling work and personal lists. The catch is the free tier: it limits active projects and removes reminders, file uploads and some filters to the paid plan. Try it free, but only pay if you genuinely use the advanced filters.

4. TickTick — best tasks-plus-habits combo

TickTick combines a to-do list with a habit tracker, calendar view and a built-in Pomodoro timer, so one app can cover your tasks, daily habits and focus sessions. The free tier is generous for everyday lists, though the calendar view and some habit statistics are limited without a subscription. Pick this one if you want tasks and habits in the same place instead of two separate apps.

5. Any.do — best calendar-and-tasks in one view

Any.do puts your tasks and calendar side by side, so you can see what is due alongside your appointments. Its free tier handles lists, reminders and recurring tasks well, and its design is one of the cleanest here. Some of the nicer extras — location reminders, WhatsApp task capture and unlimited collaboration — sit behind the paid plan, so check the free tier first before committing your lists to it.

Our verdict: install Microsoft To Do first — it is free with no paid tier waiting to limit you, and its My Day view is the easiest daily habit to build. Only move to Todoist if you need its natural-language dates and filters, and only pay for it after the free tier has actually held you back. Avoid task apps that hide reminders — the single most basic feature — behind a subscription.
