5 Best Free Keyboard Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Your keyboard sees everything you type — passwords, messages, search queries. That makes it the one app where "free" deserves a second look. We tested the most popular free keyboards on a mid-range Android phone for one question: which one types accurately, stays fast, and respects your privacy?

What we tested

  • Typing accuracy — autocorrect and predictions on everyday messages, not just perfect English
  • Swipe and voice input — glide typing and dictation that actually work
  • Speed and size — does it lag on a mid-range phone or hog storage?
  • Privacy — what the keyboard needs to phone home, and what you can switch off
  • Everyday extras — clipboard, themes, one-handed mode, multilingual typing

1. Gboard — best for most people

Google's keyboard is the default on most Android phones for a reason: fast swipe typing, accurate predictions, excellent voice typing, built-in search, GIFs and a clipboard that actually keeps your recent copies. Multilingual typing works without switching keyboards. The trade-off is the obvious one — it is a Google product, so if you want zero Google services in your keyboard, skip to OpenBoard below.

2. Microsoft SwiftKey — best predictions

SwiftKey's predictions still feel a step ahead: it learns your phrasing, handles mixed-language typing well, and its swipe typing is genuinely fast. It also syncs your learned dictionary across devices if you sign in — handy, but optional, and worth switching off if you would rather keep your typing data on one phone.

3. Samsung Keyboard — best if you already have one

If you own a Samsung phone, check the keyboard that came with it before installing anything. It covers swipe typing, voice input, clipboard and handwriting input, it is well tuned to Samsung phones, and it costs nothing extra. No download needed — it is already on your phone.

4. OpenBoard — best for privacy

OpenBoard is open-source and does not send your typing anywhere: no account, no cloud sync, no ads. It covers the essentials — swipe typing, suggestions, clipboard and themes — but its predictions are simpler than Gboard's or SwiftKey's, and voice typing depends on a separate app on your phone. Choose it when privacy matters more than smart predictions.

5. Fleksy — best for speed and themes

Fleksy is built for fast typists: big keys, strong autocorrect, gesture controls and an enormous theme library. Its prediction engine is good, though not quite as uncanny as SwiftKey's. If you are bored of your keyboard's look, or you keep hitting the wrong keys, Fleksy is the fun fix.

Our verdict: stick with Gboard if it is already on your phone — it is fast, accurate and free, and voice typing alone makes it hard to beat. Switch to SwiftKey if predictions matter most, or to OpenBoard if you want a keyboard that sends nothing anywhere. And a warning that applies to every keyboard: never install a keyboard from outside the Play Store, and never grant one full network access you do not understand — a keyboard is the worst place for a shady app.
