5 Best Free Language Learning Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Learning a new language on your phone only works if the app keeps you coming back — and that is exactly what we tested. We tried the most popular free language apps on a mid-range Android phone for one question: which one actually teaches you to use the language, without ads, a subscription, or a paywall blocking you on day two?

What we tested

  • Real learning, not just streaks — whether the lessons build usable speaking, listening and reading skills, or only test you on words you already know
  • Native speech — real people speaking the language, not only robotic text-to-speech
  • Ads & paywalls — how much of the course is genuinely free and where the free tier stops
  • Everyday use — short sessions that fit into a day, offline lessons where offered, and no account forced on you before the first lesson

1. Duolingo — best free, best for a daily habit

Duolingo is the most downloaded language app for a reason: short, game-like lessons across 40+ languages, with streaks, leagues and daily reminders that make five minutes a day genuinely stick. The core courses are free — the trade-off is ads between lessons and a hearts system that limits mistakes before you have to wait or practise to refill them. Super (the paid tier) removes the ads and the hearts limit. If you want one app that turns language learning into a daily habit you will actually keep, start here.

2. Memrise — best for hearing how people really speak

Memrise is built around short videos of native speakers in everyday situations, so you learn the language the way people actually use it — slang, speed and all — instead of textbook sentences read by a robot voice. It covers a huge range of languages, and its spaced-repetition system is good at drilling vocabulary into long-term memory. The free plan covers a limited set of vocabulary lessons and videos; Memrise Pro unlocks the full library, unlimited speaking practice and an ad-free experience. If your goal is understanding real people, this is the strongest free starting point.

3. Busuu — best for structured, proper courses

Busuu feels the most like a real language course: expert-designed lessons in 14 languages, from beginner to intermediate, with grammar explanations and a community feature where native speakers correct your written and spoken exercises. That human feedback is the thing no other app on this list offers for free. The catch is the free tier — you get the first lessons of each course, and full access needs a subscription. If you want a serious, structured path rather than a game, Busuu is the most complete option here.

4. Drops — best for building vocabulary fast

Drops does one thing and does it well: vocabulary, taught in five-minute picture games. Every word comes with an illustration and native-speaker audio, and it even teaches alphabets for languages like Korean, Japanese and Arabic. The free version gives you one five-minute session, then you wait before the next one — which is either a perfect daily limit or frustratingly short, depending on how much time you have. There is no grammar teaching here, so pair it with one of the course apps above if you want full sentences, not just words.

5. HelloTalk — best for talking to real people

HelloTalk is less a course and more a language exchange: you chat, voice-call and video-call native speakers of the language you are learning, and in return you help them with yours. Built-in translation, pronunciation and correction tools make the conversations work even at beginner level, and the community covers 150+ languages. The core exchange features are free; a VIP subscription adds extras like unlimited translations and advanced search. One honest caveat: you are talking to strangers, so use the app's reporting and blocking tools and keep personal details private. If you can already say a few sentences and want real conversation practice, nothing else on this list teaches speaking faster.

Our verdict: install Duolingo first — its free courses and daily streak system are the easiest way to build a lasting habit. Add Memrise when you want to hear how people really speak, and HelloTalk when you are ready to practise with native speakers. Busuu is the pick if you want a proper structured course, and Drops is the best five-minute vocabulary booster to use alongside any of them.
