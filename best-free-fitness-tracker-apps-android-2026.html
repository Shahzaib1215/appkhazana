5 Best Free Fitness Tracker Apps for Android (2026)

By AppKhazana Team · Updated November 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Most fitness apps are free to install and then quietly lock the useful parts behind a subscription. We looked at the most popular free fitness trackers with one question: which one actually helps you move more, without charging you for your own step count? The honest answer depends on what you do — walking, running, gym workouts and sleep tracking are different jobs, and no single free app does all of them best.

What we tested

  • Free-tier honesty — what works without paying, and what is subscription-only
  • Tracking accuracy basics — GPS routes for runs and rides, step counting from the phone's sensors
  • Everyday use — how much battery GPS tracking uses, and whether the app works without a smartwatch
  • Privacy trade-off — what account and health data each app asks for

1. Google Fit — best free all-rounder

Google Fit tracks steps, distance and active minutes (as "Heart Points") using just your phone — no watch or subscription needed. It is simple to the point of being plain, but that is the appeal: it counts your movement, shows your history, and syncs with many other fitness apps. If you just want a free, no-nonsense daily activity record, start here.

2. Strava — best for runners and cyclists

Strava's free tier records GPS runs, rides, walks and hikes with route maps, pace and splits, and it syncs with most watches and bike computers. The social feed — friends' activities, kudos and challenges — is genuinely motivating if you like training with others. Advanced analysis and route planning sit behind the subscription, but basic recording is free and fully usable.

3. Nike Run Club — best free running coach

Nike Run Club is free with no subscription tier at all: GPS run tracking, audio-guided runs led by coaches, and training plans for distances from 5K to marathon. The guided runs are the standout feature — a coach talking you through intervals beats staring at a pace screen. The catch is scope: it is a running app, not a general fitness tracker, and some training plans are only available in certain countries.

4. Samsung Health — best if you already own a Samsung phone

Samsung Health comes preinstalled on Galaxy phones and covers steps, workouts, sleep and weight in one place, with optional challenges and coach videos. Check it before installing anything else — if your phone already has it, you may not need another tracker at all. Some features work best with a Galaxy Watch, and availability varies by device and region.

5. Fitbit — best for sleep and daily habits

Fitbit's app works without a Fitbit device for basic step and activity tracking, and it shines if you do own one: sleep stages, resting heart rate trends and readiness-style summaries in one timeline. Much of the deeper analysis requires a Premium subscription, so treat the free tier as a solid daily log rather than a personal coach.

Our verdict: install Google Fit first for everyday movement — it is free, light and works on any Android phone. Add Strava if you run or cycle seriously, or Nike Run Club if you want a free coach in your ear. Skip any "free" tracker that hides your own step history behind a paywall, and remember GPS tracking drains battery — carry a charger on long outdoor workouts.
